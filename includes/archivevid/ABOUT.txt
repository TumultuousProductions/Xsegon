sets.json: sets of game videos on the Internet Archive (archive.org), one console each.

Xsegon carries this list of sets and nothing of what is in them: no video and no
file name. At a press (Get Media > Videos from online archives) it asks
the Archive for each set's own list of files, finds the video that is surely a
game's, and fetches that one file for a game that has none.

A game the sets leave without is tried against World of Longplays
(longplays.org): recordings of whole games played by its members, each on the
Archive under the collection worldoflongplays and naming its player. Xsegon
keeps the first 30 seconds of the recording's smaller copy as the game's video
(bin\ffmpeg.exe reads only that much, never the whole file) and writes the
player's name in the log. Credit to both is in README.md under Credits.

Each entry:
  id         the set's name on the Archive (archive.org/details/<id>)
  folder     the console, by the short name the video matcher knows (includes\videomatch.php)
  whose      a short mark for who put the set up and when
  hevc       true where the videos are H.265, which not every browser plays
  videos, mb how many videos it held and their size, when the list was made
  title      the set's title on the Archive

Made 2026-10-11 from the 97 sets found on 2026-10-09 (doc\ideas\43-videos-for-everyone.md).
Left out: four sets that come packed (.zip, .rar, .7z), which cannot be taken one
video at a time, and one for a console Xsegon's matcher does not know.
Left out for good: every set whose own page says it is a paid service's pack
put up again by someone else. None of those is to be added.
No set states a licence but one, and that mark is its uploader's own.
