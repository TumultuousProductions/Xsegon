list.json: the texture packs a game's details lists (the Texture packs tab), for
the consoles whose emulator here loads them: PS2 (PCSX2), GameCube and Wii
(Dolphin), N64 (RetroArch's Mupen64Plus-Next core).

It is read from the Texture Packs Archive, the community's list kept by Sad
Origami: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1sif8FeRGJRbytK8wFRXgF6Hke9V6GUFs
Anyone on that list's own Blacklist tab is left out, and so is a row filed under
a console its pack is not for (NOT_FOR in the tool: SonicBOOM's RE:Enhance mods,
which its pages say are for the PC release). A few packs the list lacks are added
from tools\texpacks-more.json, each read from its own page by a person.
`node tools\build-texpacks.js` makes this file again; it is run before a release,
never on a person's PC.

Xsegon carries this list and none of the packs. Each pack is its maker's own. A
press opens a pack's own page in the browser; for a pack whose page is on
emulationking.com, evilgames.eu or a GitHub project with a release, Xsegon
fetches that pack's own download from there, on the person's PC, at their press.
Credit is in README.md under Credits.

Each console is a list of packs:
  k       a key of the pack's own, from its console, title, maker and page
  t       the game's title as the list writes it
  name    the pack's own name, where its page gives one
  by      who made it
  kind    hand (made by hand), ai (an AI upscale), mixed, or '' (not said)
  st      Complete, Not finished, Part of the game, or '' (not said)
  r       the release it was made for: NTSC-U, PAL, NTSC-J, or ''
  bytes   its size, from its download where it has one, else as the list says
  pay     free, paid, account (needs an account to download), or ads
  page    its page; where its own host
  tip     its maker's tip page, or ''
  get     its own download Xsegon may fetch {url, bytes, name}, or null
  from    only on a row of tools\texpacks-more.json: xsegon (the tab says "not in the Archive")
  says    only on such a row: a few words the tab shows on it, or ''

A game finds a row by its title or its file's name as one key: words run
together, II to XV read as numbers ("Soulcalibur II" is "Soul Calibur 2"), and a
title of two releases joined by " + " is each of them ("Final Fantasy X +
International" is Final Fantasy X and Final Fantasy X International).
